How to use each chapter

1 · Read the idea

Each topic begins with the concept in plain language, then tightens it to the precise definition the course uses. Nothing is skipped just because it looks advanced.

2 · Move the visual

Interactive panels let you flip bits, toggle gates, push the stack, and step through fetch/execute. Seeing the register values change is the point.

3 · Work the example

Worked examples solve real exam-style problems line by line, including the reasoning behind every register value.

4 · Test yourself

Practice questions hide their solutions until you try. The practice page maps each one back to the control it resembles.

Scope note. The companion follows the supplied slides: Chapter 1 (Review of Digital Design), Chapter 2 (Top Level View of Computer), and Chapter 3 (CPU). Topics from later chapters are not taught here yet.
Exam-first tip. Past controls repeatedly test the same skills: reading a register trace, computing instruction size, decoding a hex opcode, following little-endian memory, and managing the stack. The practice page is organized around those skills rather than around years.

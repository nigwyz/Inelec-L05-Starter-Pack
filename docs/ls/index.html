How to use each chapter

1 · Read the idea

Each topic begins with the concept in plain language, then tightens it to the precise definition the course uses. Nothing is skipped just because it looks advanced.

2 · Move the visual

Interactive panels let you shift, reverse, convolve, change the ROC, re-check linearity and re-synthesise a spectrum. Seeing the stem plot change is the point.

3 · Work the example

Worked examples solve real exam-style problems line by line, including the reasoning behind every step the professor expects to see written down.

4 · Test yourself

Practice questions hide their solutions until you try. The practice page maps each one back to the control it resembles.

Scope note. The companion follows the recovered course material: Chapter 1 (DT Signals and Systems), Chapter 2 (DT LTI Systems), Chapter 3 (DT Fourier Series), Chapter 4 (DT Fourier Transform) and Chapter 5 (Z-transform). Chapter 6 (Sampling) is not included because it is not examined in the control.
Exam-first tip. The controls repeat a small set of skills: classify a signal, prove a property, convolve two sequences, synthesise a periodic signal from its coefficients, apply Parseval, and read a Z-transform with its ROC. The practice page is organised around those skills rather than around years.

The “n+1 terms” rule. A convolution sum is always finite. If you find yourself summing an infinite geometric series, the number of overlapping terms is not what you think it is.

6DFT

X[k] = Σn=0N−1 x[n] WNkn,   x[n] = (1/N) Σk=0N−1 X[k] WN−kn,   WN = e−j2π/N
FactStatement

Relation to DTFTX[k] = X(e^{j2πk/N}) — samples of the DTFT at N points
PeriodicityX[k+N] = X[k]

Radix-2 FFT. A length-N = 2^m DFT costs N log₂N complex multiplies instead of N². The decimation-in-time butterfly is X = E + WNkO, X' = E − WNkO, where E and O are the even- and odd-indexed sub-DFTs.

System function. H(z) = Y(z)/X(z). Poles are the roots of the denominator, zeros the roots of the numerator. Causal ⇔ ROC outside the outermost pole. Stable ⇔ the ROC contains the unit circle. Causal and stable ⇔ all poles strictly inside the unit circle.

8Inverse Z-transform

MethodWhen to use itHow
Table lookupthe transform is a standard pairmatch the ROC too
Partial fractionsrational H(z) with simple polessplit into A/(1−pz⁻¹) terms, then invert each with its ROC
Power serieslog, e^z, non-rationalexpand in z⁻¹ (right-sided) or z (left-sided) and read the coefficients

Check your answer. h[0] = limz→∞H(z) for a causal system, and the ROC must contain the unit circle if the system is stable. If both fail, the partial fractions are wrong — not the ROC.

Set A2024–25 control · 15 points · 1 h 30

This is the newest control. It is almost entirely Chapters 1–3: conversion, flux, charge integration, and point-charge fields. Four short questions carry 3 points; the three exercises carry 12.

Part 1 — Short questions (3 pts)

1.1 · 0.5 pt
Express the uniform vector field F = 10x̂ in (a) cylindrical coordinates and (b) spherical coordinates. State the point dependence.
Show solution

At a point with azimuth φ, x̂ = cosφ ρ̂ − sinφ φ̂. Therefore

F = 10(cosφ ρ̂ − sinφ φ̂)

For spherical, x̂ = sinθ cosφ r̂ + cosθ cosφ θ̂ − sinφ φ̂. Therefore

F = 10[sinθ cosφ r̂ + cosθ cosφ θ̂ − sinφ φ̂]

Why it matters: the components depend on φ (and θ), even though the original field is uniform. A vector field is not a list of constants; it is a rule attached to each point.

1.2 · 0.5 pt
Give the equation form of Gauss's law for the electric field, both integral and differential.
Show solution
∮S D·dS = Qenc = ∫V ρv dv   and   ∇·D = ρv

The surface is closed and the normal points outward. The differential form is the pointwise version obtained by the divergence theorem. If the medium is linear, D=εE.

1.3 · 1 pt
A charge Q = 30 nC moves at 2 × 10⁶ m/s along v̂ = 0.60x̂ + 0.75ŷ + 0.30ẑ in B = 2x̂ − 4ŷ + 3ẑ mT. Find the magnitude of the magnetic force.
Show solution

First, v = 2×10⁶(0.60, 0.75, 0.30) = (1.20, 1.50, 0.60)×10⁶ m/s.

Compute the cross product (B in mT = 10⁻³ T):

v×B = [(1.50)(3) − (0.60)(−4)]x̂ − [(1.20)(3) − (0.60)(2)]ŷ + [(1.20)(−4) − (1.50)(2)]ẑ ×10⁶×10⁻³
= (6.90x̂ − 2.40ŷ − 7.80ẑ) ×10³

Then

F = Q v×B = 30×10⁻⁹ × (6.90, −2.40, −7.80)×10³ = (207, −72, −234) μN
|F| = √(207² + 72² + 234²) ≈ 320.6 μN

Check: F is perpendicular to both v and B. The dot products with v and B are zero (up to rounding).

1.4 · 1 pt
A filamentary wire of infinite length carries current I along the z-axis in free space. Give the magnetic flux density at any point in the xy-plane.
Show solution
B = (μ₀I/(2πρ)) φ̂   (T)

with μ₀ = 4π×10⁻⁷ H/m. The field forms concentric circles; the direction follows the right-hand rule with the thumb along the current. In components, φ̂ = (−y x̂ + x ŷ)/ρ.

Exercise 1 — Flux and divergence theorem (6 pts)

Exercise 1 · 6 pts
Given D = 3r² r̂ + (12φ/π) φ̂ C/m², evaluate the flux of D across the closed surface defined by 1 ≤ r ≤ 2, 0 ≤ θ ≤ π, π/4 ≤ φ ≤ π/2. Then verify the result with the divergence theorem.
Show solution

Step 1 — identify the four active surfaces. The region is a spherical wedge. The θ=0 and θ=π faces have zero area because dS ∝ sinθ = 0. The active faces are r=1 (normal −r̂), r=2 (normal +r̂), φ=π/4 (normal −φ̂), and φ=π/2 (normal +φ̂).

Step 2 — r=1 face. dS = r² sinθ dθ dφ r̂ = sinθ dθ dφ r̂ at r=1. Also D·r̂ = 3.

Φ₁ = −3 ∫₀^π sinθ dθ ∫_{π/4}^{π/2} dφ = −3(2)(π/4) = −3π/2

Step 3 — r=2 face. D·r̂ = 3(4)=12, dS = 4 sinθ dθ dφ.

Φ₂ = 12·4 ∫₀^π sinθ dθ ∫_{π/4}^{π/2} dφ = 48(2)(π/4) = 24π

Step 4 — φ=π/4 face. dS = r dr dθ (−φ̂), and D·(−φ̂) = −12φ/π = −3 at φ=π/4.

Φ₃ = −3 ∫₁² r dr ∫₀^π dθ = −3(3/2)(π) = −9π/2

Step 5 — φ=π/2 face. D·φ̂ = 12φ/π = 6.

Φ₄ = 6 ∫₁² r dr ∫₀^π dθ = 6(3/2)(π) = 9π

Step 6 — add.

Φ = −3π/2 + 24π − 9π/2 + 9π = 27π ≈ 84.8 C

Step 7 — divergence theorem. In spherical coordinates,

∇·D = (1/r²)∂(r²·3r²)/∂r + (1/(r sinθ))∂(12φ/π)/∂φ
= (1/r²)(12r³) + 12/(π r sinθ) = 12r + 12/(π r sinθ)

The volume element is dv = r² sinθ dr dθ dφ. The first term:

∫ 12r dv = 12(∫₁² r³ dr)(∫₀^π sinθ dθ)(∫_{π/4}^{π/2} dφ) = 12(15/4)(2)(π/4) = 45π/2

The second term:

∫ 12/(π r sinθ) dv = (12/π)(∫₁² r dr)(∫₀^π dθ)(∫_{π/4}^{π/2} dφ) = (12/π)(3/2)(π)(π/4) = 9π/2

Sum = 54π/2 = 27π. The two sides agree.

Marks live in: the sign of the inner r face, the normal on each φ face, and the sinθ factor. Write the normal vector next to every integral before you integrate.

Exercise 2 — Total charge (2 pts)

Exercise 2 · 2 pts
In free space, ρ_v = (ρ₀/a) r cosθ C/m³. Find the total charge within (a) the sphere r = a; (b) the cone r ≤ a, 0.9π ≤ θ ≤ π.
Show solution

(a) Full sphere.

Q = ∫₀^a∫₀^π∫₀^{2π} (ρ₀/a) r cosθ · r² sinθ dφ dθ dr
= (ρ₀/a)(a⁴/4)(2π) ∫₀^π cosθ sinθ dθ

But ∫₀^π cosθ sinθ dθ = [sin²θ/2]₀^π = 0. Hence Q = 0. The positive and negative hemispheres cancel exactly.

(b) Cone. The φ integral is still 2π, and the r integral is still a⁴/4. Only the θ limits change:

Q = (ρ₀/a)(a⁴/4)(2π) ∫_{0.9π}^{π} cosθ sinθ dθ
= (ρ₀a³π/2)[sin²θ/2]_{0.9π}^{π}
= (ρ₀a³π/2)(0 − sin²(0.9π)/2)

sin(0.9π)=sin162°≈0.3090, so sin²/2≈0.04775.

Q ≈ −0.0750 ρ₀a³ C

Check: the cone is in the lower hemisphere (θ near π), where cosθ<0, so a negative charge is expected.

Exercise 3 — Electric field of two charges (4 pts)

Exercise 3 · 4 pts
Let Q₁ = 60 nC at P₁(4,−2,7) and Q₂ = 60 nC at P₂(4,−7,−2). (a) Find E at P₃(1,2,3) in free space. (b) Find the points on the y-axis where E_z = 0.
Show solution

(a) Field at P₃. The vectors from each source to P₃ are

R₁ = P₃ − P₁ = (−3, 4, −4),   R₁ = √41
R₂ = P₃ − P₂ = (−3, 9, 5),   R₂ = √115

With 1/(4πε₀)=9×10⁹ and Q=60×10⁻⁹, the common factor is 540:

E = 540[ R₁/41^{3/2} + R₂/115^{3/2} ]
= 540[ (−3,4,−4)/262.5 + (−3,9,5)/1233.3 ]
≈ 540(−0.01143 + −0.00243, 0.01524 + 0.00730, −0.01524 + 0.00405)
≈ (−7.48 x̂ + 12.17 ŷ − 6.04 ẑ) V/m

The handwritten key gives −7.48x̂ + 12.16ŷ − 6.0ẑ V/m; the difference is rounding.

(b) Points on the y-axis. Put P=(0,y,0). Then

R₁ = (−4, y+2, −7),   R₁² = (y+2)² + 65
R₂ = (−4, y+7, 2),   R₂² = (y+7)² + 20

The z-component is

E_z = 540[ −7/R₁³ + 2/R₂³ ] = 0
⇒ 7R₂³ = 2R₁³

Raise both sides to the 2/3 power:

7^{2/3}[(y+7)²+20] = 2^{2/3}[(y+2)²+65]

With 7^{2/3}=3.659 and 2^{2/3}=1.587, this becomes

1.305y² + 28.28y + 90.1 = 0
y = [−28.28 ± √(28.28² − 4·1.305·90.1)]/(2·1.305)
y₁ ≈ −3.88,   y₂ ≈ −17.84

Check: both points are on the negative y side, between/beyond the two charges. Substituting either back gives E_z≈0 to rounding.

Time budget for this paper. 1.3 minutes per short-question point, 8–10 minutes for the flux setup, 10 for the volume integral, 20 for the two-charge field. If the flux integral is taking longer than 20 minutes, you are probably fighting the limits, not the physics.

Set B2021–22 control · flux, field, Stokes

The older control uses the same spine with different numbers: a long flux + divergence-theorem exercise, a point-charge field, and a Stokes verification. Use it as a transfer test after the 2024–25 set.

Exercise 1 · 10 pts
Given A = ρ cosφ ρ̂ + 3 sinφ φ̂ + (z+1) ẑ in the region 1 ≤ ρ ≤ 2, 0 ≤ φ ≤ π/6, 0 ≤ z ≤ 5. The flux through the top surface z=5 is 3π/2. (a) Evaluate the flux over the closed surface. (b) Verify with the divergence theorem. (c) Compute ∇f for f=(∇·A−1)/cosφ.
Show solution

(a) Six faces. The region is a cylindrical wedge. The faces are ρ=1, ρ=2, φ=0, φ=π/6, z=0, z=5.

FaceNormalA·n̂dSFlux
ρ=1−ρ̂−cosφdφ dz−5 sin(π/6) = −2.5
ρ=2+ρ̂2cosφ2 dφ dz4·5·0.5 = 10
φ=0−φ̂0dρ dz0
φ=π/6+φ̂3 sin(π/6)=1.5dρ dz1.5·1·5 = 7.5
z=0−ẑ−1ρ dρ dφ−(3/2)(π/6) = −π/4
z=5+ẑ6ρ dρ dφ3π/2 (given)
Φ = −2.5 + 10 + 0 + 7.5 − π/4 + 3π/2 = 15 + 5π/4

(b) Divergence theorem.

∇·A = (1/ρ)∂(ρ·ρcosφ)/∂ρ + (1/ρ)∂(3sinφ)/∂φ + ∂(z+1)/∂z
= 2cosφ + (3cosφ)/ρ + 1 = cosφ(2 + 3/ρ) + 1

The volume integral is

∫ [cosφ(2+3/ρ)+1] ρ dρ dφ dz
= 15 + 5π/4

which matches the surface sum.

(c) Gradient. First, ∇·A − 1 = cosφ(2+3/ρ), so f = 2 + 3/ρ. Then

∇f = (∂f/∂ρ)ρ̂ = −(3/ρ²)ρ̂
Exercise 2 · 3 pts
For B = −y x̂ + x ŷ − 2.5 ẑ, evaluate ∮B·dl around the circle x=a₀+r₀cos t, y=b₀+r₀sin t, then verify with Stokes's theorem.
Show solution

Line integral. dx=−r₀sin t dt, dy=r₀cos t dt. On the circle,

B·dl = (−y)(−r₀sin t)dt + x(r₀cos t)dt
= r₀[(b₀+r₀sin t)sin t + (a₀+r₀cos t)cos t]dt
= r₀[b₀sin t + a₀cos t + r₀]dt

Integrating 0 to 2π kills the sine and cosine terms:

∮B·dl = 2πr₀²

Stokes. The curl is

∇×B = (∂B_y/∂x − ∂B_x/∂y)ẑ = (1 − (−1))ẑ = 2ẑ

The disk area is πr₀² and dS=ds ẑ:

∫(∇×B)·dS = 2πr₀²

Both sides agree. The −2.5ẑ term does not contribute because it is perpendicular to the path.

The 2-point field question on this paper is the same skill as 2024–25 Exercise 3. Do that one twice instead of memorizing a second geometry.

Set CRecitation transfers

These are the professor's recitation problem types, rewritten with clean numbers. If the control throws a different geometry, this is where the transfer comes from.

Vectors · dot and projection
Given M(5,2,1), N(−7,0,2), P(2,−8,3). Find MN, MP, their dot product, the scalar projection of MN on MP, the angle between them, and the vector projection.
Show solution
MN = (−12, −2, 1),   MP = (−3, −10, 2)
MN·MP = 36 + 20 + 2 = 58
|MN| = √149 ≈ 12.21,   |MP| = √113 ≈ 10.63
cosθ = 58/(12.21×10.63) ≈ 0.4469 ⇒ θ ≈ 63.4°
scalar projection = 58/10.63 ≈ 5.46
vector projection = (58/113)(−3,−10,2) ≈ (−1.54, −5.13, 1.03)
Coordinates · cylindrical → Cartesian
Express F = (cosφ ρ̂ + sinφ φ̂)/ρ² in Cartesian coordinates. Evaluate it at A(1,0,0) and B(1,−1,−3).
Show solution

Use F_x = cosφ F_ρ − sinφ F_φ and F_y = sinφ F_ρ + cosφ F_φ:

F_x = (cos²φ − sin²φ)/ρ² = cos2φ/ρ²
F_y = (2sinφcosφ)/ρ² = sin2φ/ρ²

With cos2φ=(x²−y²)/ρ², sin2φ=2xy/ρ², and ρ²=x²+y²:

F = [(x²−y²)x̂ + 2xyŷ]/(x²+y²)²

At A(1,0): F = x̂. At B(1,−1): ρ²=2, x²−y²=0, 2xy=−2 ⇒ F = (0, −0.5, 0).

Gradient · three systems
Find ∇f for (a) f=x²y−y³; (b) g=2sinφ−ρz+4; (c) h=2r cosθ−5φ+2.
Show solution

(a) Cartesian:

∇f = 2xy x̂ + (x² − 3y²)ŷ

(b) Cylindrical:

∇g = (−z)ρ̂ + (2cosφ/ρ)φ̂ + (−ρ)ẑ

(c) Spherical:

∇h = 2cosθ r̂ − 2sinθ θ̂ − (5/(r sinθ))φ̂
Divergence theorem · box
Evaluate both sides of the divergence theorem for D = 2xy x̂ + x² ŷ over the box 0≤x≤1, 0≤y≤2, 0≤z≤3.
Show solution

∇·D = 2y.

∫∇·D dv = ∫₀¹∫₀²∫₀³ 2y dz dy dx = 3·4 = 12

Surface: x=0 gives 0; x=1 gives ∫2y dy dz = 12; y=0 gives −∫x² dx dz = −1; y=2 gives +1; the z faces give 0 because D_z=0.

∮D·dS = 12
Divergence theorem · shell
For D = 3r² r̂, evaluate both sides of the divergence theorem in the region between the spherical shells r=1 and r=2.
Show solution

Surface: at r=2, D=12, area 16π ⇒ 192π. At r=1, the outward normal is inward, D=3, area 4π ⇒ −12π. Total 180π.

Volume: ∇·D=12r, dv=4πr²dr:

∫₁² 12r·4πr² dr = 48π[r⁴/4]₁² = 48π(15/4) = 180π
Gauss · cube with zero divergence
Given D = 2(x+y)x̂ + (3x−2y)ŷ C/m², find ρ_v, the charge in the cube 0≤x,y,z≤2, and verify with the surface integral.
Show solution
ρ_v = ∇·D = ∂[2(x+y)]/∂x + ∂(3x−2y)/∂y = 2 − 2 = 0

So Q=0. The surface integral also cancels: the x faces give +∫2(2+y)dydz = 24 at x=2 and −∫2y dydz = −8 at x=0; the y faces give +∫(3x−4)dxdz = −4 at y=2 and −∫3x dxdz = −12 at y=0. Sum 24−8−4−12=0.

Ring and disk · on axis
(a) A ring of radius b carries total charge Q. Find E on its axis. (b) An annular disk with inner radius a, outer radius b, and surface density ρ_s lies in the xy-plane. Find E_z on the axis.
Show solution

(a) Ring. Every element is at the same distance √(b²+z²) from P. The perpendicular components cancel; the axial component is multiplied by z/√(b²+z²):

E_z = Q z/(4πε₀(b²+z²)^{3/2})

(b) Annular disk. Integrate rings of radius r with charge dq=ρ_s 2πr dr:

dE_z = (ρ_s 2πr dr) z/(4πε₀(r²+z²)^{3/2})
E_z = (ρ_s z/(2ε₀)) ∫_a^b r/(r²+z²)^{3/2} dr
= (ρ_s/(2ε₀))[ z/√(a²+z²) − z/√(b²+z²) ]

For a full disk, set a=0: E_z=(ρ_s/(2ε₀))(1 − z/√(b²+z²)).

Biot–Savart · infinite wire
An infinite filament carries 15 A along the z-axis. Find H and B at (a) A(20,0,4) and (b) B(−2,−4,12).
Show solution

For a z-directed wire, H = I/(2πρ) φ̂ with φ̂ = (−y x̂ + x ŷ)/ρ.

(a) At A: ρ=20, φ̂=ŷ.

H = 15/(40π) ŷ ≈ 0.119 ŷ A/m,   B = μ₀H ≈ 0.150 ŷ μT

(b) At B: ρ=√20≈4.472, φ̂=(4x̂−2ŷ)/4.472=(0.894x̂−0.447ŷ).

H ≈ 0.534(0.894x̂ − 0.447ŷ) = (0.477x̂ − 0.239ŷ) A/m
B = μ₀H ≈ (0.600x̂ − 0.300ŷ) μT

Set DFinal-scope drill

If your exam is the final rather than the control, these four cover the Chapter 4–6 patterns: continuity, Faraday, wave parameters, and skin depth.

Continuity
Given J = (x²x̂ − 4y²ŷ + 2xzẑ)cosωt A/m², find the volume charge density ρ_v.
Show solution
∇·J = (2x − 8y + 2x)cosωt = (4x − 8y)cosωt

Continuity: ∂ρ_v/∂t = −∇·J.

ρ_v = −∫(4x−8y)cosωt dt = ((8y−4x)/ω) sinωt + C

The constant is set by the initial charge distribution; for a neutral initial state, C=0.

Faraday
A circular loop of radius a lies in the xy-plane in B=B₀cosωt ẑ. Find the induced emf and the azimuthal electric field.
Show solution
Φ_B = B₀πa²cosωt
emf = −dΦ_B/dt = πa²B₀ω sinωt

By symmetry, E is azimuthal and constant on the loop:

∮E·dl = E_φ(2πa) = πa²B₀ω sinωt
E_φ = (aB₀ω/2) sinωt

Lenz: the induced current opposes the change in flux, so its direction reverses every half-cycle.

Plane wave
A uniform plane wave travels in a material with μ_r=3 and β=36π rad/m. The scanned paper header says 300 MHz, but the official solution uses ω=2π×900 MHz. Use f=900 MHz (the set that matches the key) and find ε_r, η, and λ.
Show solution

β=ω√(με)= (ω/c)√(μ_rε_r), so the refractive index is

n = √(μ_rε_r) = βc/ω = (36π·3×10⁸)/(2π·9×10⁸) = 6
ε_r = n²/μ_r = 36/3 = 12
η = η₀√(μ_r/ε_r) = 377√(3/12) = 377/2 ≈ 188.5 Ω
λ = 2π/β = 2π/(36π) = 1/18 m ≈ 5.56 cm

Source warning. The scan header says 300 MHz, but the official arithmetic divides by 9×10⁸, i.e. 900 MHz; the next page writes ω=2π×900 MHz. At 300 MHz the same β would give ε_r=108 and η≈62.8 Ω, while λ stays 5.56 cm because β is given. In the exam, state the frequency you substitute and keep one consistent set.

Skin depth
Find the skin depth of copper at 1 MHz. Take σ=5.8×10⁷ S/m, μ_r=1.
Show solution
δ = 1/√(πfμσ) = 1/√(π·10⁶·4π×10⁻⁷·5.8×10⁷)
= 1/√(2.289×10⁸) ≈ 6.61×10⁻⁵ m ≈ 0.066 mm

At 10 MHz, δ shrinks by √10 to about 0.021 mm. This is why high-frequency current stays near the surface.
